# Next recordings for the agentic robotics library

Use an ordinary phone or glasses video. No checkerboard or extra equipment is required for the current visual pipeline. A recorded dimension would help a later metric simulation, but the browser task must continue to label unmeasured scale as unknown.

## Best next example: phone and USB-C cable

Record one clear 20–40 second take. Begin with the phone and connector end apart, briefly show the port and plug, connect them slowly, pause, disconnect and show both parts again. Keep the interaction large in frame, lighting steady and your fingers away from the mating surfaces when possible. A short view of each part before and after occlusion gives segmentation and identity tracking useful evidence.

The useful new library content is a rigid phone, a connector end, an identified port, demonstrated approach and separate/inserted visual states. Cable shape remains deformable. A visually inserted plug does not establish power, charging or electrical contact.

The first browser task can use authored port/plug frames with explicit provenance once reviewed. Reliable simulator insertion additionally needs compatible scale, collision geometry, insertion clearances and a force/contact model. The workbench should expose these requirements rather than treating a fitted visual mesh as a connector specification.

## Best articulated example: cupboard door or drawer

Start closed, open slowly, pause, close, then repeat once with less hand occlusion. Include the whole moving panel and enough stationary cabinet to establish a parent frame. A short change of viewpoint before or after the operation is helpful, but avoid fast camera movement during the critical opening.

This tests whether the pipeline distinguishes a revolute door from a prismatic drawer, preserves the attachment and estimates a repeatable motion axis. The browser robot can inspect that relationship; a physical robot still needs a handle/grasp frame, joint resistance and collision validation.

## Adapter and receptacle

For a purely visual example, an unplugged power strip and adapter can supply the separate parts and insertion sequence. Keep it a separate library task from charging a phone: the sequence contains two different mating relationships and a flexible cable. The system must preserve the difference between visible insertion, mechanical engagement and electrical function.

## Acceptance checks before a new twin becomes public

- Every visible moving component has a stable identity and an exact source-bound native asset, or an explicit missing-geometry status.
- A hinged or sliding part retains its parent connection through the complete replay. Missing observations remain unknown.
- The descriptive object program distinguishes observed states, model proposals and authored robot-task anchors.
- The native meshes, source video and connected replay can be inspected together. Failed identity, geometry or joint estimates stay private for review.
- The robotics compiler advertises only operations supported by that twin. A missing connector or an unresolved joint blocks the requested operation instead of substituting another example.

One successful new interaction is more useful than a large library of unreviewed objects. Start with phone/USB-C or one clear door, then use the owner feedback loop to improve the general pipeline.
